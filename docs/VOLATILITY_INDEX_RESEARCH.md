# Volatility Index Research

## V75 liquidation playbook (2026-09-22) — built, tested, does not survive replication

The user's brief: trade V75 like a professional — real setups, TP/SL, trade the
spots where people got liquified. Built two full engines
(`analysis/vol75_strategy_v2.py`, `analysis/vol75_ltf_grabs.py`) and a
liquidation-spot profiler. **Liquidation spot = wick through a stale swing
level** (M5 fractal K=3 or M1 fractal K=2, untouched >=15 bars-24h before the
grab). Conservative accounting: loss wins stop/target ties, 1x spread per RT.

**What a V75 liquidation spot actually looks like (the map):**
- ~25 grabs/day of stale M1 levels (R_75); ~70-93% RECLAIM (close back inside
  within 3 bars). The trap event is real and robust.
- But the forward path after a reclaim carries no tradable magnitude
  (fwd15 mean ≈ ±10-50u vs 14.79u spread = noise). Liquidity events exist;
  the aftermath is drift, not snapback.
- Grabs do NOT continue either: join-the-break lost in **every** window and
  symbol tested (-1.3x to -3.5x spread, 10+ windows) — though partly a
  stop-geometry artifact (break entries have tiny risk distances, so 1x
  spread is a proportionally huge tax).

**The one that nearly made it:** fade-the-grab (reclaim -> trade opposite,
stop beyond sweep extreme +/- 0.5 ATR, 1.0-1.5R target). 30d in-sample:
n=692, WR 55-65%, **+1.7x to +2.9x spread** — the first V75 config with a
real in-sample edge, and friction is tiny here (spread ≈ 9% of stop distance).
OOS: 2/4 windows positive (+2.0x/+4.3x on 0d/12d; -1.3x/-0.3x on 5d/19d),
1HZ75V negative, and family replication (R_10, R_25, 1HZ10V/50V/100V, 11
windows) flat noise everywhere except 1HZ50V — which printed +90x spread in
one window and -29x in the adjacent one. Sign-flipping windows = drift legs
borrowing the strategy's clothes, same mechanism that killed the FVG pattern.

**Verdict: closed.** The playbook is real; the instrument has no liquidity
physics for it to grip. Economics at $0.50-risk sizing would have been
~$0.20/trade, ~23 trades/day — worth nothing since the edge isn't real. Engines remain
reusable as-is for any instrument. The one honest positive: V75's robust
facts (clockwork cadence, symmetric generator, high reclaim rates) are now
measured, not assumed.: Is There Any Ownable Structure? (Sep 21, 2026)

**Question:** the Volatility (R_) family is a different instrument class from
Boom/Crash/Flip — continuous, allegedly constant-volatility PRNG markets. The
Sep 10 universe scan only tested *persistence/phase-follower* logic on them.
Does the generator leave any *other* ownable structure (mean reversion, runs,
vol clustering, time-of-day artifacts, tick-scale asymmetries), and is it
ownable net of spread at $10-scale sizing?

**Verdict up front: no. Three independent data views agree — the Vol family is
efficiently random at every accessible scale, and the spread is a pure toll
with no offsetting asymmetry to charge it against.** This *confirms by
contrast* what makes flip1000 special: its edge is a designed asymmetry
(spike generator), which is exactly what the Vol family lacks.

## Ground truth about the instrument

- Generated by a cryptographically-secure PRNG at nominally **constant
  volatility** (the 10/25/50/75/100 label = annualized vol target); 24/7, no
  sessions, no news (Deriv docs).
- Empirical cadence is **clockwork**: (1s) family = exactly 1 tick/s, R_10 =
  exactly 1 tick/2s. Zero gaps >2s in 24h. The feed never stalls server-side.
- **Jump symmetry**: mean up-jump / mean down-jump = 0.994–1.006 across the
  family. There is no Boom/Crash-style event to fade — `spike_bars = 0`
  everywhere in the Sep 10 scan.

## The spread hurdle, quantified

The (1s) family has the *lowest friction in the entire Deriv universe*
($0.0006–0.0038/RT at min lot per the Sep 10 scan; spread ≈ 0.33–0.45× a
typical 1-min bar move) — and it still kills everything, because the edge it
must clear is ~zero:

- Any tick-scale conditional edge is bounded by the median tick jump
  (0.085–0.12u); live spread is **~3× the median jump**. Breaking even on a
  next-tick directional bet requires **>75% directional accuracy**; measured
  accuracy is 50.0% ± 0.2.
- Bar-scale reversal edge measured −2 to −5u per trade vs 0.38–0.59u spread.

## Data-quality warning for future work

The stage-1 72h bar cache (`data/synth_scan/bars_*.npy`, 4,320 rows/symbol)
carries a **−0.5 minute-close autocorrelation artifact** (likely open/close
interleaving in that fetch pipeline). The 30-day cache (`bars30_*`) and direct
tick pulls agree with each other and show the true AC1 ≈ 0. **Use `bars30_*`
or fresh API pulls for any Vol-family study; do not trust `bars_*`.**

## Standing conclusion

The Vol family is now *measured*, not assumed: no momentum, no reversion, no
vol clustering, no seasonality, no tick-scale asymmetry — every scale returns
the coin flip the generator is designed to produce, at every sampling
frequency we can access (1s ticks → 30d bars). Combined with the Sep 10 scan
(persistence logic: same@3 ≈ 0.5, all configs net-negative), the class is
closed for the $10→$100 mission. **flip1000 @5.0 min_age=1 remains the sole
vehicle; its spike asymmetry is the thing this family measurably lacks.**

*Caveats: 24h of ticks + 30d of bars is short for regime claims, but the null
hypothesis here is the generator's own specification (constant-vol PRNG), not
a market regime; a true anomaly would have to be a deliberate generator
artifact, which the symmetry tests would surface. Spreads sampled Sep 10.*

## Addendum: the SMC/ICT lens (FVGs, structure, BOS/CHoCH, sweeps) — Sep 21 late

**Question (user):** re-examine the family through trader concepts — Fair
Value Gaps, HH/HL/LH/LL structure, BOS/CHoCH, liquidity sweeps — instead of
statistical moments. Tool: `analysis/vol_smc_scan.py` (every concept is a
testable sequence operation; shuffle + return-permutation surrogates as
controls), `analysis/vol_fvg_followup.py` / `analysis/vol_fvg_adversarial.py`
(replication).

**One candidate emerged — and died on replication.** Bull-FVG first-touch
SHORT on R_10/R_25 M1: in-sample z=-5.08 vs a 20-seed return-permutation
null; replicated on 4 symbols in a second window (z -2.95..-5.17); naked-run
control said the gap itself mattered (+-0.75u over impulse-only shorts). But
across four windows the effect exists only in the two most recent (adjacent,
one regime cluster) and is absent/sign-flipped at -12d and -19d
(z +1.01..-0.84, net $-0.27..+$0.05/event):

| Window | R_25 net/event | z | R_10 net/event | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| today (IS) | — | -5.08 | — | -5.08 |
| -5d | +$0.22 | -5.17 | +$0.07 | -4.20 |
| -12d | -$0.27 | -0.78 | -$0.20 | -0.84 |
| -19d | -$0.02 | +1.01 | +$0.05 | +1.05 |

Reading: the generator's random walk produces multi-hour drift legs; ANY
'short the strength' heuristic (FVG touch, naked run, CHoCH) profits during
down-legs and loses during up-legs. The permutation null preserves each
window's return SET but destroys clustering, so it over-credits sequence
patterns inside trending windows. Across regimes the effect averages to zero
- exactly what an efficient PRNG market must do.

**Other concepts, same verdict (real-vs-surrogate, all ~null):** structure
persistence HH->HH / LL->LL z -1.7..+1.3 (trend labels don't persist);
BOS/CHoCH forward edges ~0.002-0.11u vs 0.19-0.60u spreads (0.01-0.22x);
FVG fill rates ARE high (70-88% touch@20) but carry no directional payload
after the touch; liquidity sweeps (SFP) cannot even exist at native cadence -
1s bars on 1HZ symbols have one tick each, hence no wicks; on aggregated M1
sweep returns equal the surrogate null (-0.44 real vs -0.43 surr).

**Standing conclusion unchanged, now covered on the SMC lens too:** no
ownable structure in the Vol family under EITHER framework. The SMC exercise
adds a mechanism insight: apparent pattern edges on this family are
regime-leg artifacts, and window-count (not p-values within one window) is
the decisive test. flip1000's spike asymmetry remains the only designed edge
in the repo's universe.

## Addendum 2: the full HTF->LTF confluence playbook (Sep 21, ~23:45 UTC)

**Question (user):** not concepts in isolation - the composite playbook: HTF
liquidity map (unmitigated M15 swings) -> LTF sweep of that pool -> rejection
-> displacement FVG -> limit entry -> HTF-target exit. Tool:
`analysis/vol_smc_confluence.py` (full engine + random-level control +
min-lot $ economics).

**Result: the playbook is starved, not profitable.** 30 days of M1 yields
~1-2 setups/symbol (V30 (1s): 25 HTF pools -> 22 swept -> 7 rejected in 5
bars -> 3 displacement FVGs -> 2 entries, both stopped; V100: 10 pools, ZERO
swept in 30d). n is too small to test - but the starvation itself is the
finding, for a principled reason:

> On a memoryless PRNG, an HTF swing high is not liquidity - it is a past
> random number. Unmitigated-for-30d means the walk has not returned there
> (common for a driftless walk), NOT that orders are parked there. Real SMC
> works because real participants anchor stops/triggers at old structure;
> the generator has no record of its own history (VR=1.00, ACF=0, symmetric
> jumps) - so HTF levels carry no causal status and confluence has nothing
> to condition on.

**Where the playbook COULD bite: Boom/Crash/Flip.** Those generators have
causal path structure (spike phases, absorption asymmetry, phase memory) -
structure there reflects generator state, not coincidence. The confluence
engine is instrument-agnostic and should be run on flip1000/Boom/Crash tick
archives before the SMC framework is judged on this repo's universe.
